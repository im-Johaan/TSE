Hear one voice in a crowd

Target speaker extraction: give the model a few seconds of someone's voice, and it pulls only that person out of a recording where 2–4 people talk at the same time. It works on clips and live, in real time.

How it works

A time-domain extraction network: the mixture is encoded into learned features, a speaker embedding computed from the reference recording steers every separation stage, and a mask keeps only the target.

Training. LibriMix (LibriSpeech read speech, 8 kHz): 251 training speakers, mixtures of 2–4 speakers generated on the fly with random levels and partial overlap.
Loss. Scale-invariant SDR of the extracted voice, plus speaker classification of the embedding and an interference-reconstruction term.
Live mode. The model runs on a sliding 3 s window every 200 ms with 100 ms lookahead; outputs are level-matched and crossfaded.

Errors are mostly the model following the wrong person when two voices sound alike. The jointly trained speaker encoder confuses unseen speakers more often than a pretrained one (speaker-verification EER 6.1% vs 2.0% for ECAPA-TDNN on the same 40 test speakers), which motivated the experiments below.

Experiments on the speaker cue

Done

More data variety and depth

Dynamic 2–4 speaker mixing and a deeper separator (R=3 → 6) cut the share of weak outputs on 3 speakers from 46% to 18% and on 4 speakers from 77% to 24%.

Stopped

Pretrained ECAPA as the speaker encoder

Frozen ECAPA-TDNN instead of the jointly trained encoder. The separator memorised the 251 training speakers: after 12 epochs 2.3 dB and 29% wrong-speaker outputs on unseen speakers, vs 5.6 dB and 8% for the baseline at the same point.

Running

ECAPA as a "judge" + hard pairs

Fine-tuning with deliberately similar-sounding interferers and a loss in which frozen ECAPA checks that the output sounds like the target and unlike the others, against a control without the judge. Results pending.
